### 1.1 Core Monetization Engines
1. **Engineering Segment (Treatment Solutions & Turnkey EPC)**: Accounts for ~60% of revenues. Designs, constructs, and commissions industrial wastewater treatment, Zero Liquid Discharge (ZLD), sea-water reverse osmosis (SWRO), and ultrapure water plants. Highly capital-intensive, execution-heavy, and susceptible to fixed-price contract margin shocks.
2. **Chemicals Segment (Resins & Specialty Membranes)**: Accounts for ~30% of revenues. Net profit engine of the company. Formulates patented ion exchange resins (*INDION*), specialty water treatment chemicals, and biocides for thermal power, refineries, and high-purity pharmaceutical manufacturing. Higher gross margins (~35–40%) and steady recurring consumable demand.
3. **Consumer Products Segment (Zero-B)**: Accounts for ~10% of revenues. Sells point-of-use domestic water purifiers, softeners, and alkaline filters. Historically operates at negligible margins or modest operating losses due to high advertising, distribution, and warranty overheads competing against Kent and Eureka Forbes.

### 1.2 Structural Moat Analysis (7 Powers Audit)
* **Switching Costs (Chemicals / Consumables - Helmer Power #5)**: Moderate to strong in the resins business. Once an industrial boiler, pharma drug formulation line, or semiconductor fabrication plant qualifies an *INDION* ion resin batch, shifting to another supplier requires re-validation under US FDA and cleanroom purity protocols.
* **Lack of Pricing Power in EPC (The Engineering Drag)**: The engineering segment operates in open competitive bidding tenders against domestic (Thermax, VA Tech Wabag, Praj, Triveni) and international (Veolia, SUEZ) contractors. Fixed-price EPC structures leave Ion Exchange unprotected against raw material inflation (stainless steel, specialized membranes, titanium valves) and extended client sign-off delays.
* **Process Power Deficit**: Operating margin has historically oscillated between 7% and 12%, but recently plunged below 3% during execution bottlenecks and ERP/SAP transition friction, demonstrating inadequate process moats to protect operational profitability during downturns.
