### 1.1 Core Monetization & Clinical Engines
1. **High-Acuity Super-Specialities (Oncology, Neurosciences & Cardiac Sciences)**:
   - High-margin complex surgical interventions contributing ~55-60% of total hospital revenues.
   - Rapid expansion into dedicated comprehensive cancer care (Linear Accelerators, PET-CT) and robotic surgical suites (Da Vinci systems), which yield ARPOBs in excess of ₹45,000–₹50,000 per day.
2. **General Specialties & Emergency Trauma**:
   - Orthopaedics & Joint Replacement, Critical Care, Nephrology, Gastroenterology, and Pulmonology.
   - Generates consistent anchor patient footfalls and high bed turnover (Average Length of Stay: ~3.5 to 3.8 days).
3. **Outpatient & Diagnostic Services (OPD & Pharmacy)**:
   - High-margin in-house diagnostic laboratories, radiology imaging, and hospital outpatient pharmacies providing recurring non-bed cash flow.

### 1.2 Structural Moat Analysis (7 Powers Audit)
* **Scale Economies & Procurement Leverage**: As operational bed capacity scaled from 1,405 beds in FY24 to over 2,800+ operational and signed beds in FY26-27, Yatharth has unlocked aggressive volume discounts on medical implants, surgical consumables, stents, and pharmaceutical supplies, maintaining industry-leading EBITDA margins of 24–26%.
* **Network Density & Local Monopolies**: In Noida and Greater Noida, Yatharth operates a dominant cluster of over 1,145 beds across three strategic micro-locations. This cluster density creates a formidable entry barrier for competing greenfield hospital chains, while facilitating shared super-specialist clinician coverage and patient referrals.
* **Process Power (Brownfield Turnaround Playbook)**: Unlike peers who rely purely on expensive greenfield builds with 4–5 year gestation cycles (costing ₹1.0–₹1.5 Cr per bed), Yatharth's acquisition and turnaround playbook (e.g., Asian Fidelis Faridabad, Model Town Delhi) acquires underutilized or stressed hospital infrastructure at ₹40–₹60 Lakhs per bed, modernizes the medical technology, deploys anchor clinicians, and achieves operational EBITDA breakeven within 9 to 12 months.
* **Anti-Disintermediation Assessment**: Physical tertiary healthcare is immune to digital platform disintermediation. Complex surgeries, emergency polytrauma, ICUs, and oncology radiation require certified physical facilities, OT infrastructure, and specialized medical teams. Upstream device suppliers and downstream corporate insurance panels cannot bypass the hospital delivery network.

### 2.1 Payer Mix & Concentration Breakdown
Historically, Yatharth maintained an elevated share of government healthcare schemes (Ayushman Bharat / PMJAY, CGHS, ECHS), which accounted for ~40% of revenues. While this ensured rapid bed occupancy in new units, it created working capital friction (debtor days of 120–125 days).

Management has initiated a deliberate, structural transformation to pivot the payer mix toward higher-margin private channels:

* **New Facility Capping**: In newly acquired hospitals (Faridabad Sector-20 and Model Town New Delhi), government scheme business is capped at 0% to 15%, ensuring day-one ARPOB realizations of ₹38,000 to ₹40,000+.

### 3.3 Management Commentary on Forensic Anomalies & Red Flags
* **Anomaly 1: Debtor Days Expansion (113 days) & Receivables Compounding**:
  * *Investigation & Management Commentary*: In recent earnings calls, management clarified that trade receivables expanded from ₹128 Cr in FY23 to ₹374 Cr in FY26 primarily due to the ~40% contribution from government panels (CGHS, ECHS, Ayushman Bharat), where verification and treasury clearance cycles typically require 110 to 125 days. Importantly, bad debt write-offs remain virtually non-existent ($<0.3\%$ of sales) as government claims carry sovereign creditworthiness. Furthermore, management is systematically de-risking this by restricting government scheme beds to 0–15% in newly launched units (Faridabad, Model Town, Agra), which is targeted to compress group debtor days toward 80–100 days.
* **Anomaly 2: Negative Free Cash Flow (-₹112 Cr in FY26)**:
  * *Investigation & Management Commentary*: The negative FCF is a natural function of high-return growth capital expenditure rather than operational cash burn. Fixed assets and CWIP expanded from ₹442 Cr in FY24 to ₹1,352 Cr in FY26 as the company acquired and commissioned over 1,200 new beds. Operating cash flow expanded robustly to ₹205 Cr in FY26 (CFO/PAT of 1.21x), demonstrating that existing beds generate immense self-funding cash flow.
* **Anomaly 3: Jun 2026 PAT Growth Deceleration (+7.1% YoY vs +52.3% Sales)**:
  * *Investigation*: In Q1 FY27 (Jun 2026), quarterly revenue jumped +52.3% YoY to ₹393 Cr, but PAT grew +7.1% to ₹45 Cr. This divergence was driven by upfront fixed overheads, clinician onboarding, interest costs (₹7 Cr vs ₹0 Cr), and a surge in depreciation (+86.7% YoY from ₹15 Cr to ₹28 Cr) following the commissioning of newly acquired facilities. As occupancy in these new beds scales from initial 45-50% toward optimal 65-70%, operating leverage will drive significant profit catch-up.
